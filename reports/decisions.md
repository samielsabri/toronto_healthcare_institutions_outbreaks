# Methodological decisions log

Decisions D1–D7 were made after exploring the data, D8–D11 while building the dashboard.

| # | Question | Decision | Rationale |
|---|---|---|---|
| D1 | Where do the "Shelter" and "Transitional Care" settings go? | **Transitional Care → hospital** (subtype "transitional care"). **Shelter → kept in the cleaned data but flagged out of scope** and excluded from analyses. | Transitional care units are hospital-operated; the same units at 200 Church St are coded "Hospital-Chronic Care" in other years, so the label looks like coding drift. Shelter is 3 Seaton House outbreaks (2016–18) and is not a healthcare institution. |
| D2 | Merge "Norovirus-like" (to 2022) with "Norovirus" (2023 on)? | **Merge** into the norovirus group. The raw label is retained. | The label switches cleanly between years and durations are similar. Methods will note that "Unable to identify" rose from 48% to 62% of enteric outbreaks at the switch, so the label change may also reflect a change in what was called norovirus. |
| D3 | Same unit, same onset date, different respiratory agent (19 pairs): one outbreak or two? | **Keep as published** (two outbreaks). The pairs are flagged in the cleaned data. | Matches the source and TPH's published counts; the effect is at most about 1% of rows in any year. Methods will note that co-detections were more often recorded within one row before 2020 and as separate rows after. |
| D4 | With co-detections, which agent does an outbreak count under? | **Every agent detected** for per-agent series and onset alerts. The stacked agent-mix chart shows co-detections as a separate **"multiple agents"** band so each outbreak is counted once. | Per-agent series answer "how many outbreaks involved this pathogen?". Counting by first-listed agent only loses over a third of RSV outbreaks in some pre-2020 seasons, and the field order is arbitrary (COVID-19 is always listed first). |
| D5 | What count as a "facility"? | **Physical site** (normalized address), **split by setting** where one address holds more than one setting (e.g. an LTCH and a retirement home). The unit-level label is kept too, and we don't consider operators. | Recurrence is about a building, its residents and its staff. Operator names change and merge over time. Co-located LTCHs and retirement homes are separately licensed with separate residents. |
| D6 | Pre-pandemic baseline window | **2016/17, 2017/18, 2018/19, plus 2019/20 up to week 10 of 2020** (season week 28). 2015/16 excluded (partial: data start January 2016). | 2019/20 tracks earlier seasons until week 10, then shows a testing spike (weeks 11–12) and a collapse in non-COVID outbreaks. Methods will note that the baseline rests on 3–4 seasons. |
| D7 | Week 53 | **Merge week 53 into week 52** for season-on-season charts. | Keeps every season on one 52-week axis. With surveillance weeks (D10) it affects 2020/21 (23 outbreaks) and 2025/26 (38). 2025/26 is a post-pandemic comparison and MEM training season, so its week 52 slot holds two weeks of onsets. |
| D8 | Repeat outbreaks: half of LTCHs with an outbreak recorded 6+ in 2025/26, because each ward counts separately. How should recurrence be counted? | **Group into episodes.** Outbreaks at the same facility, of the same type (respiratory, enteric, other), each beginning within **14 days** of the previous one form one episode; a longer gap starts a new episode. An episode belongs to the season of its first onset. Recurrence = 2+ episodes in a season. Unit-level counts are kept alongside. | Several wards declared together are one event, not repeats. 14 days is about two respiratory incubation periods plus reporting lag, and matches the window used to size unit splitting in the EDA. Applies to the facility page only; all other counts remain outbreaks as published. |
| D9 | Season-onset alerts: which method? | **Moving epidemic method (MEM)** for influenza and RSV, with the `mem` package's **default settings**, fitted on the post-pandemic seasons (2022/23 to the last complete season) using weekly outbreaks involving the agent (D4 counting), all settings. Onset = first complete week above the epidemic threshold (MEM default); the dashboard also reports consecutive weeks above it. **COVID-19: no formal alert**; shown against the post-pandemic range. | MEM is the standard for respiratory season onset (ECDC, WHO Europe). COVID-19 has no stable seasonal pattern yet. Limitations stated on the page: only 4 training seasons, and small weekly counts make the threshold low (about 2 outbreaks a week for influenza), so a single week above it can be noise. |
| D10 | Which week definition? | **Surveillance (CDC MMWR) weeks, Sunday to Saturday**; week 1 contains 4 January. The season runs from week 35 to week 34. | This is the definition Toronto Public Health, Public Health Ontario and PHAC's FluWatch use, so a given week number covers the same days as in their reports. With it, this pipeline reproduces the institutional outbreak counts on TPH's Integrated Respiratory Diseases Dashboard exactly (week 45 of 2026/27: influenza 7 → 5, season to date 30; COVID-19 season to date 32). Replaces ISO weeks (Monday to Sunday), used until November 2026. |
